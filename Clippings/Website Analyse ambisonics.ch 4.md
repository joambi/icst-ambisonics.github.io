## ICST Ambisonics Philosophy

*Field-Based Spatial Composition and Decoding*

---

## 1\. Why Another Ambisonics Platform?

Ambisonics is often presented as a neutral encoding format — mathematically elegant, flexible, and format-independent.

At the **Institute for Computer Music and Sound Technology**, Ambisonics is not treated as a neutral container.

It is treated as:

> A compositional field model.

This distinction defines our philosophy.

---

## 2\. From Channel-Based to Field-Based Thinking

Conventional spatial production paradigms (stereo, 5.1, Atmos) are channel-based systems.  
They define loudspeaker targets.

Ambisonics, however, describes a continuous spatial sound field using spherical harmonics.

At ICST, we adopt a strict field-based perspective:

- The B-format is the primary artistic representation.
- Loudspeaker arrays are rendering devices — not compositional targets.
- Decoding is interpretation, not translation.

This leads to fundamentally different design decisions.

---

## 3\. The Problem with “Neutral” Decoding

Standard decoding approaches (e.g. ALLRAD-style vector-based methods) optimize geometric energy distribution.

They are mathematically consistent.

However, studio practice revealed recurring perceptual phenomena:

- Depth blurring in complex scenes
- Frequency-dependent spatial diffusion
- Reduced vertical precision in practical arrays
- Instability when translating between layouts

These observations motivated a re-evaluation of decoding assumptions.

---

## 4\. The ICST Multi-Band Field Approach

The ICST Ambisonics system introduces a perceptually informed strategy:

### Core Principles

1. **Field coherence over speaker precision**
2. **Frequency-dependent spatial structuring**
3. **Layered decoding architectures**
4. **Array-aware rendering**
5. **Studio-validated perceptual testing**

Instead of a single global decoding matrix, the ICST MultiDecoder architecture allows:

- Independent decoding layers (e.g., height / mid / low field)
- Frequency-dependent weighting
- Controlled spatial focus vs. diffusion
- Adaptation to non-symmetric arrays

This does not abandon Ambisonics theory.  
It extends it toward perceptual practice.

---

## 5\. Ambisonics as Composition, Not Only Technology

At ICST, Ambisonics is not primarily an engineering problem.

It is a compositional space.

The decoder becomes:

- A spatial instrument
- A timbral shaping tool
- A depth-sculpting mechanism
- A field articulation device

This perspective emerges from real studio production and performance contexts, not purely theoretical models.

All ICST Ambisonics tools are developed and tested in real production environments at the:

- Zurich University of the Arts

- Composer feedback

The system evolves through practice.

## 7\. Beyond Plugin Thinking

The ICST Ambisonics Decoder is not a standalone plugin.

It is part of an integrated system:

Encoding → Spatial Structuring → Multi-Layer Decoding → Array Rendering

This systemic view distinguishes our work from isolated plugin implementations.

## 8\. A Critical Position

We do not consider Ambisonics a solved technology.

Questions we actively investigate:

- How does decoding influence perceived depth?
- Can frequency-layered decoding enhance spatial clarity?

Ambisonics at ICST is:

## The ICST Ambisonics Philosophy

**Field-Based Spatial Composition and Perceptually Informed Multi-Layer Decoding**

---

## Abstract

Ambisonics is commonly understood as a mathematically elegant, format-independent spatial audio representation based on spherical harmonics. While widely adopted for its flexibility and compatibility with arbitrary loudspeaker configurations, its practical implementation in compositional and studio contexts often relies on geometrically optimized decoding strategies that prioritize energy distribution over perceptual structuring.

This paper outlines the philosophical and methodological framework underlying the Ambisonics research and development conducted at the Institute for Computer Music and Sound Technology (ICST), Zurich University of the Arts. We propose a field-based and perceptually informed interpretation of Ambisonics, in which decoding is understood not as a neutral translation process but as an interpretative rendering layer. This approach led to the development of a multi-layer, frequency-aware decoding architecture implemented in the ICST Ambisonics system.

---

## 1\. Introduction

Ambisonics describes a continuous three-dimensional sound field using spherical harmonic components. In contrast to channel-based formats (e.g., stereo, 5.1, object-based systems), it encodes directional information independently of specific loudspeaker arrangements.

Standard decoding strategies—such as energy-preserving or vector-based approaches—are typically designed to achieve geometric consistency and energy distribution accuracy across loudspeaker arrays. These methods optimize measurable criteria such as localization vectors and amplitude panning coherence.

However, extended studio practice at ICST revealed perceptual phenomena that are insufficiently addressed by purely geometric optimization, including:

- Reduced perceptual depth differentiation in dense spatial textures
- Frequency-dependent spatial diffusion
- Instability of vertical imaging in non-ideal arrays
- Translation inconsistencies between playback systems

These observations motivated a re-examination of decoding assumptions within compositional and production environments.

---

## 2\. From Channel Paradigms to Field-Based Composition

Conventional spatial production models define space through discrete reproduction channels. Ambisonics, by contrast, represents a continuous field independent of reproduction geometry.

At ICST, Ambisonics is therefore treated as:

> A compositional field model rather than a channel delivery format.

In this perspective:

- The B-format constitutes the primary artistic representation.
- Loudspeaker arrays function as rendering devices.
- Decoding becomes a perceptual interpretation layer.

This shift redefines the role of the decoder from a mathematically optimal transformation to an acoustically and perceptually informed rendering instrument.

---

## 3\. Decoding as Interpretative Rendering

Geometrically optimized decoding (e.g., energy-preserving or vector-based solutions) assumes that mathematical optimality correlates with perceptual clarity. While effective in many scenarios, empirical listening sessions conducted in ICST studio environments indicated that:

- Spatial sharpness and depth articulation are frequency-dependent.
- Low-frequency content often dominates spatial perception differently than high-frequency components.
- Vertical localization is highly sensitive to spectral distribution and array topology.
- Homogeneous decoding matrices may reduce perceptual contrast between spatial layers.

These findings suggest that decoding strategies can benefit from perceptually informed differentiation rather than uniform global matrices.

---

## 4\. Multi-Layer and Frequency-Aware Decoding Architecture

The ICST Ambisonics system introduces a multi-layer decoding architecture characterized by:

1. Independent decoding layers (e.g., lower, mid, upper spatial zones)
2. Frequency-dependent weighting strategies
3. Array-aware adaptation
4. Controlled diffusion versus spatial focus
5. Studio-based perceptual validation

Instead of applying a single global decoding matrix across the entire frequency spectrum, spatial rendering can be structured into differentiated bands. This approach allows:

- Enhanced depth articulation
- Clearer separation of vertical spatial layers
- Increased robustness across irregular arrays
- Artistic control over spatial density

Importantly, this architecture does not abandon Ambisonics theory. It remains grounded in spherical harmonic representation while extending decoding toward perceptually motivated structuring.

---

## 5\. Studio-Embedded Research Methodology

The development of the ICST Ambisonics framework is embedded in compositional and performance practice within the ICST 3D composition studio.

Research methodology includes:

- Iterative listening evaluations
- Comparative decoding analysis
- Translation tests across multiple loudspeaker configurations
- Composer feedback integration
- Practical constraints from live performance contexts

This practice-based research model situates technical development within artistic application rather than abstract simulation alone.

---

## 6\. Ambisonics as a Compositional Instrument

Within this framework, the decoder is reconceptualized as:

- A spatial articulation instrument
- A depth-sculpting mechanism
- A timbral-spatial shaping tool
- A field-structuring device

Spatial rendering is not treated as a transparent final step but as an active component of compositional design.

This perspective encourages composers to consider decoding strategies as aesthetic parameters rather than fixed technical processes.

---

## 7\. Critical Position and Open Questions

The ICST Ambisonics philosophy maintains that Ambisonics is not a technologically “solved” domain. Ongoing research addresses questions such as:

- To what extent does geometric optimization correlate with perceptual precision?
- Can frequency-layered decoding improve depth stability?
- How does Higher-Order Ambisonics behave under practical array constraints?
- What are the aesthetic implications of decoding choices?
- How can Ambisonics translation between systems be stabilized?

These questions frame Ambisonics as an evolving research field rather than a finalized standard.

---

## 8\. Conclusion

The ICST Ambisonics philosophy proposes a field-based, perceptually informed and studio-embedded interpretation of Ambisonics. By reconceptualizing decoding as interpretative rendering and implementing multi-layer frequency-aware architectures, the ICST system bridges mathematical representation with compositional and perceptual practice.

Ambisonics is thus approached not merely as a spatial encoding format but as a living compositional paradigm.

## ABSTRACT

Ambisonics is widely regarded as a format-independent spatial audio representation based on spherical harmonics, offering flexibility across arbitrary loudspeaker configurations. In practice, however, many implementations rely on geometrically optimized decoding strategies that prioritize energy preservation and localization vector accuracy. Extended studio practice at the Institute for Computer Music and Sound Technology (ICST), Zurich University of the Arts, revealed perceptual limitations in such approaches, particularly regarding depth articulation, vertical precision, and translation stability across playback systems.

This paper outlines a field-based and perceptually informed framework for Ambisonics decoding developed at ICST. Decoding is reconceptualized as an interpretative rendering layer rather than a neutral transformation. A multi-layer, frequency-aware decoding architecture is introduced, embedded within compositional and performance practice. The approach integrates psychoacoustic considerations, array-aware adaptation, and studio-based evaluation to bridge mathematical representation with perceptual and artistic objectives.

---

## 1\. INTRODUCTION

Ambisonics encodes a three-dimensional sound field using spherical harmonic components, separating spatial description from specific reproduction geometries. Unlike channel-based paradigms (e.g., stereo, 5.1, object-based systems), Ambisonics represents spatial information as a continuous field.

Standard decoding strategies—such as energy-preserving, max-rE, or vector-based approaches—are typically designed to optimize measurable criteria including localization vectors, amplitude coherence, and energy distribution. These methods are mathematically consistent and widely adopted.

However, extended production and compositional practice at ICST identified perceptual phenomena not fully addressed by purely geometric optimization:

- Reduced perceptual depth differentiation in dense spatial scenes
- Frequency-dependent diffusion of spatial images
- Instability of vertical localization in non-ideal arrays
- Inconsistent translation between different loudspeaker configurations

These observations motivated a reconsideration of decoding as a perceptual and compositional process rather than solely a geometric one.

---

## 2\. FROM CHANNEL-BASED TO FIELD-BASED COMPOSITION

Conventional spatial production frameworks define space via discrete reproduction channels. In contrast, Ambisonics describes a continuous acoustic field independent of loudspeaker layout.

At ICST, Ambisonics is therefore treated as a **field-based compositional model**:

- The B-format constitutes the primary artistic representation.
- Loudspeaker arrays function as rendering devices.
- Decoding is understood as perceptual interpretation rather than direct translation.

This shift reframes the decoder from a mathematically optimized transformation matrix to an acoustically and perceptually informed rendering instrument.

---

## 3\. DECODING AS INTERPRETATIVE RENDERING

Geometric optimization assumes that mathematical precision correlates directly with perceptual clarity. While this assumption holds in many controlled scenarios, empirical listening sessions conducted in the ICST 3D composition studio indicate that:

- Spatial sharpness varies significantly across frequency bands.
- Low-frequency components contribute differently to spatial impression than high-frequency content.
- Vertical localization is particularly sensitive to spectral balance and array geometry.
- Uniform decoding matrices may reduce perceptual contrast between spatial layers.

These findings suggest that decoding strategies can benefit from perceptually informed differentiation across frequency and spatial domains.

---

## 4\. MULTI-LAYER AND FREQUENCY-AWARE DECODING ARCHITECTURE

The ICST Ambisonics system implements a multi-layer decoding architecture characterized by:

1. Independent decoding layers corresponding to spatial zones (e.g., lower, middle, upper regions)
2. Frequency-dependent weighting strategies
3. Array-aware adaptation mechanisms
4. Controlled balance between spatial focus and diffusion
5. Iterative validation in studio-based listening environments

Rather than applying a single global decoding matrix across the entire spectrum, spatial rendering is structured into differentiated frequency bands. This approach enables:

- Enhanced depth articulation
- Improved vertical separation
- Increased robustness across irregular loudspeaker arrays
- Artistic control over spatial density and clarity

Importantly, this architecture remains grounded in spherical harmonic representation and does not depart from Ambisonics theory. Instead, it extends decoding toward perceptually motivated structuring.

---

## 5\. STUDIO-EMBEDDED RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

The development of the ICST framework is embedded within compositional practice and performance production in the ICST 3D composition studio at the Zurich University of the Arts.

Methodological components include:

- Iterative perceptual listening sessions
- Comparative evaluation of decoding strategies
- Translation tests across multiple array geometries
- Composer feedback integration
- Live performance validation

This practice-based research model situates technical innovation within artistic application rather than isolated simulation environments.

---

## 6\. AMBISONICS AS COMPOSITIONAL INSTRUMENT

Within this framework, the decoder assumes an expanded role:

- Spatial articulation instrument
- Depth-sculpting mechanism
- Timbre-space interaction tool
- Field-structuring device

Spatial rendering becomes an active compositional parameter rather than a transparent finalization stage. This perspective encourages composers and researchers to treat decoding strategies as aesthetic variables.

---

## 7\. DISCUSSION AND OPEN QUESTIONS

The ICST approach does not consider Ambisonics a technologically finalized domain. Ongoing research investigates:

- The relationship between geometric optimization and perceptual precision
- The impact of frequency-layered decoding on depth stability
- Higher-Order Ambisonics behavior under practical array constraints
- Aesthetic implications of decoding design decisions
- Translation stability across heterogeneous playback systems

These questions frame Ambisonics as an evolving field requiring continued integration of mathematical modeling, psychoacoustics, and compositional practice.

---

## 8\. CONCLUSION

This paper presents a field-based and perceptually informed interpretation of Ambisonics developed at ICST. By reconceptualizing decoding as interpretative rendering and implementing multi-layer, frequency-aware architectures, the ICST system bridges spherical harmonic representation with perceptual and compositional objectives.

Ambisonics is thereby approached not merely as a format-independent encoding scheme but as an evolving compositional paradigm embedded in studio practice.

## 4\. COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS OF DECODING PARADIGMS

## 4.1 Energy-Preserving and max-rE Decoding

Energy-preserving decoders aim to maintain total acoustic energy consistency between the spherical harmonic domain and the loudspeaker domain. These methods optimize for global amplitude coherence and typically minimize mean-square error between the encoded field and reproduced pressure distribution.

The max-rE (maximum energy vector) approach improves directional sharpness by modifying higher-order components to maximize the length of the energy vector. This increases localization precision for dominant sources and is widely applied in Higher-Order Ambisonics (HOA) systems.

While these methods are mathematically robust and effective in idealized geometries, several limitations emerge in practical studio environments:

- The optimization criteria remain primarily geometric rather than perceptual.
- Spatial sharpness improvements may be accompanied by increased spectral coloration.
- Depth perception is not explicitly modeled.
- Vertical localization remains highly dependent on array symmetry.

In particular, max-rE optimization can produce enhanced frontal localization at the expense of spatial homogeneity across the field.

---

## 4.2 ALLRAD and Vector-Based Approaches

The ALLRAD (All-Round Ambisonic Decoding) paradigm combines virtual loudspeaker projection with vector-based amplitude panning to accommodate irregular loudspeaker arrays. It provides:

- Flexibility for non-uniform speaker distributions
- Improved localization stability in asymmetric setups
- Compatibility with arbitrary layouts

ALLRAD effectively separates decoding into two stages:

1. Projection onto a virtual regular array.
2. Redistribution via vector-based panning (e.g., VBAP-like methods).

While this hybrid approach enhances geometric adaptability, it introduces conceptual and perceptual consequences:

- The field representation is partially reduced to a discrete panning framework.
- Vector-based redistribution prioritizes point-source localization over continuous field coherence.
- Spectral and depth-related perceptual effects remain unaddressed.

From a field-theoretical perspective, ALLRAD implicitly shifts the rendering logic toward source-based reconstruction rather than continuous field articulation.

---

## 4.3 Perceptual Observations in Studio Practice

Extensive evaluation in the ICST 3D composition studio revealed perceptual phenomena not sufficiently captured by existing paradigms:

1. **Frequency-Dependent Spatial Diffusion**  
	High-frequency content often exhibits greater apparent localization precision than low-frequency components, independent of decoding matrix design.
2. **Depth Blurring in Dense Textures**  
	Uniform decoding matrices tend to reduce contrast between near and far spatial layers.
3. **Vertical Instability**  
	Height perception degrades disproportionately when speaker elevation angles deviate from ideal spherical distributions.
4. **Translation Variability**  
	Decoders optimized for geometric energy preservation may exhibit unstable perceptual translation between arrays of differing topology.

These observations suggest that geometric optimality does not necessarily imply perceptual optimality.

---

> The optimization target is perceptual field articulation rather than geometric energy preservation.
