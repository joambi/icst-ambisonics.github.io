This template is not a rigid protocol but a scaffold: it offers questions, not answers. Whether you work with pencil on paper, type notes live during the session, or simply keep the categories in mind — it can be adapted to your own working style. It connects directly to the methodology and case studies in [Work Analyses](/composing-in-ambisonics/085-work-analyses/).

**After the session**

Comparing notes with another listener is worthwhile: in a full-sphere system, no two seats receive identical inter-aural information — divergences in observation are not errors but evidence of the spatial complexity of the work. The session notes in the [ASCOLTA Listening Guide](/blog/ascolta-listening-guide/) work through many of these steps for specific pieces from the ASCOLTA programme — a useful companion for putting this template into practice.

For a curated repertoire of works to use with this template, see the [Repertoire Guide for Spatial Composition](/composing-in-ambisonics/0855-repertoire-guide/).
