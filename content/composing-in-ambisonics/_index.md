Ambisonics gives composers access to a three-dimensional sound field as compositional material: not as an effect added later, but as a structural dimension alongside pitch, rhythm, and timbre. Movement, depth, envelopment, elevation, and the relationship between point-like and diffuse sound objects can all be **composed**.

This section is not a technical archive, but a **workspace for composition**. It brings together three perspectives that belong together in practice:

- **listen**: how spatial music can be analysed and described
- **contextualise**: how FOA, HOA, studios, concert spaces, and tools fit together historically and practically
- **shape**: how spatial parameters, spatial counterpoint, and toolchains translate into actual compositional decisions

## Three Good Entry Points

- **I am new to Ambisonics**: start with **Overview**, then **Analytical Listening** and the **10 Questions in 3D Space**.
- **I already compose and need spatial strategies**: go directly to **Spatial Counterpoint**, **Spatial Parameters**, and **Comparing Compositional Spatial Strategies**.
- **I need a practical workflow quickly**: start with **Tools and Software**, then **Studio Practice at ICST** and **Binaural Delivery / Archiving**.

## If You Only Have 30 Minutes

Read **Overview** first, then the **10 Questions in 3D Space**, and decide from there:

- Need a **listening vocabulary**? Continue with **Analytical Listening**.
- Need a **compositional model**? Continue with **Spatial Counterpoint**.
- Need a **setup path**? Continue with **Tools and Software**.
