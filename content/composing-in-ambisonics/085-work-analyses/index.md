Analytical listening and compositional theory remain abstract until they are tested against concrete pieces. This chapter therefore connects the concepts developed so far — the parameter taxonomy from the spatial parameters chapters, the categories of analytical listening, the psychoacoustic principles, and the four compositional spatial strategies — into a practical analytical method, and demonstrates it through two structured case studies.

The case studies are designed as **model analyses**: they do not analyse specific works in the manner of scholarly secondary literature, but reconstruct two typical spatial dramaturgies from the electroacoustic repertoire, showing which categories and questions allow such pieces to be heard with analytical clarity. A commented repertoire guide at the end of the chapter provides concrete listening suggestions — from the ASCOLTA programme, ICST residencies, and the [3D AudioSpace platform](https://audiospace.soundingfuture.com/) by Sounding Future.

A useful orientation throughout is Denis Smalley's typology of spatial experience: **source-bonded space** (space attached to a recognisable sound object — its position, distance, movement); **perspectival space** (the listener's imagined position within an acoustic environment — room size, distance, vantage point); and **spectral space** (space that emerges from timbral and textural distribution rather than from localisation). These three categories — developed in "Space-form and the acousmatic image" (2007) — form a direct analytical counterpart to the parameter table used here. For a listener-oriented companion to this chapter, with exercises, session-specific examples, and Smalley's categories as its central framework, see the [ASCOLTA Listening Guide](/blog/ascolta-listening-guide/).

## Analytical Methodology: An Integrated Approach

A productive work analysis for spatial composition operates on three levels simultaneously:

**Level 1 — Parametric observation:** What is audible? Where are sources located? How do they move? How are distance and diffusion deployed? This level is descriptive and close to the heard experience. The parameter table from [Analytical Listening](/composing-in-ambisonics/02-analytical-listening/) is useful here: azimuth, elevation, distance, spread, movement, layering.

**Level 2 — Strategic classification:** Which of the four compositional spatial strategies shape the piece? Does space serve voice separation, movement dramaturgy, envelopment, or perspective — or do these strategies alternate? This level is interpretive and asks about the formal framework.

**Level 3 — Psychoacoustic assessment:** How plausible are the heard effects from a psychoacoustic standpoint? Are spatial cues (ITD, ILD, D/R ratio, envelopment structure) deployed consistently or deliberately inconsistently? This level asks about the quality of spatial perception — what is reliable, what is fragile.

The three levels are not independent. An observation on level 1 (e.g. "the source moves quickly from left to right") leads on level 2 to the question of whether this movement has motivic or structural significance, and on level 3 to the question of whether the speed of the ITD shift remains localisable.

## Case Study A: Spatial Transformation — Field Sound and Synthetic Envelopment

**Typical scenario:** A piece for 3D Ambisonics (at least 3rd order) that departs from field recordings and transforms them into synthetic spatial fields. Performance context: dome or large loudspeaker array.

### Phase 1 — Exposition (0:00–2:00): Spatial Orientation

The piece opens with a clearly localisable field recording. One or two discrete sources are placed on the horizontal plane; azimuth and elevation are stable, distance is relatively near. The D/R ratio is high — direct sound dominates, little diffusion.

**Analytical observation:** Localisation is precise because ITD and ILD are consistent. Low diffusion maintains the sound object as a clear gestalt. Smalley's source-bonded space — space as a property of the object itself — fully dominates here.

**Compositional function:** Familiarity anchor. The listener orients and builds a spatial expectation. This phase works with the **voice-separation** strategy at a minimal level: one source, one space.

### Phase 2 — Transformation (2:00–5:30): Destabilisation and Expansion

The field recording begins to change. Spectrally: high-frequency components are filtered, lending the sound distance. Spatially: the spread parameter increases; the source loses its point-like character and begins to extend across multiple regions of the sphere. Simultaneously, diffuse layers appear in the rear and upper areas.

**Analytical observation:** The transition from source-bonded to perspectival space is clearly audible. The source loses its clear gestalt; instead of ITD-based localisation, diffuse spatial energy and reflection structure shape the listening. The D/R ratio falls; LEV (listener envelopment) begins to dominate.

**Compositional function:** The transformation is not only timbral but spatially constitutive. The piece uses the psychoacoustic threshold between object and field perception as a formal boundary. The transition is precisely controllable: spread and D/R ratio are the actual dramaturgy parameters.

### Phase 3 — Immersion (5:30–8:00): Full Envelopment

The original source is barely audible as such. The field is now fully diffuse — no clear direction, maximum LEV, minimal localisation. Overhead layers and lower regions are active. Perception shifts from "I hear something there" to "I am within something."

**Analytical observation:** This phase works exclusively with the **texture and envelopment** strategy. The psychoacoustics of LEV — late lateral reflections, decorrelation — become the actual compositional parameter. Spatial resolution has become irrelevant for localisation; now the temporal structure of reflection density determines the effect.

**Compositional function:** Climax through spatial completeness, not through loudness.

### Phase 4 — Withdrawal (8:00–10:00)

A new discrete source emerges from the envelopment — or the original source returns as a point. Distance decreases, spread falls, localisation returns. The piece ends with a clear gestalt, but in an altered context.

**Overall analytical assessment:** This dramaturgy uses the psychoacoustic transition between object-perception and field-perception as its primary formal principle. Cue consistency varies deliberately between phases — precise in phase 1, deliberately inconsistent in phase 3. This is compositionally coherent because it is grounded in an actual psychoacoustic mechanism.

Concrete listening examples for this type: Philip Samartzis's *Atmospheres & Disturbances* ([ASCOLTA #5](/blog/ascolta/05-ascolta/), ICST Residency 2019) transforms field recordings from the Jungfraujoch high-alpine station into synthetic spatial fields across a 28-minute arc. Hans Tutschku: *Remembering Japan*, Part 1 *Immersion* ([ASCOLTA #9](/blog/ascolta/09-ascolta/), ICST Residency 2022) follows the same dramaturgy in a more concentrated form. Youngjae Cho: *cycle: incarnations – II. narthex* ([ASCOLTA #19](/blog/ascolta/19-ascolta/), ICST Residency 2025) works at a lighter material scale — a park that gradually destabilises from familiar nearness and returns as acoustic memory. Ágnes Máthé: *Fluidum* ([3D AudioSpace](https://audiospace.soundingfuture.com/tracks/fluidum)) traces the same three-part arc (Arrival — Exploration — Emergence) with the composer's own water recordings as source material; characteristically, space is used as a structuring parameter from the very first stage of composition — not added afterwards.

## Case Study B: Spatial Counterpoint — Three Voices and a Shared Space

**Typical scenario:** A piece for 3D Ambisonics that works with two to four clearly distinguishable sound objects, treating their spatial relations as core compositional material. Performance context: binaural or medium dome array.

### Opening Configuration

Three sources: one percussive (short impulses), one harmonic (drone, spectrally stable), one noise-like and diffuse (broadband, texture-like). Starting positions: percussive left-front, drone right-back, noise overhead-centre.

**Initial analytical question:** Are these three voices maintained as independent lines throughout the piece — or do they dissolve into one another? And how is this process spatially controlled?

### Structure and Spatial Voice-Leading

The piece opens with clear spatial separation: all three sources are positioned so that no two are close together. This is a classic application of the **voice-separation** strategy: azimuth distances of at least 60–90° keep the voices readable as separate lines.

Over the course of the piece, a gradual approach begins. The percussive source and the drone converge horizontally. Analytically relevant here is the question: at what azimuth separation does listening tip from "two voices" to "one sound"? This threshold varies with HOA order (sharper orders allow closer voice-leading), with timbral difference (very similar sounds collide sooner), and with movement speed.

In the middle section: crossing. The percussive source travels from left to right, crossing the path of the drone voice. This is spatial contrary motion in the sense of the **contrapuntal vocabulary** from the Spatial Counterpoint chapter. The crossing generates a moment of heightened spatial tension — both voices briefly share the same spatial point before drifting apart.

Concrete listening examples for this type:

Mario Mary: *Polyphonic Philosophy* ([ASCOLTA #11](/blog/ascolta/11-ascolta/)) is explicitly conceived as a "melody of sound objects" and "polyphony of space" — a study in how horizontal voice-leading constitutes formal structure. Mary's *Pedro en su laberinto* (also [ASCOLTA #11](/blog/ascolta/11-ascolta/)) shows the same idea in denser form: multiple trajectories layer without fusing.

Juliana Herrero: *FUGA N22* ([3D AudioSpace](https://audiospace.soundingfuture.com/tracks/fuga-n22)) pursues an explicitly fugal voice logic: "open and closed lines — flat and three-dimensional — multiplicities and dichotomies engage in dialogues with space and time." Inspired by John Cage's imaginary landscapes, the piece operates with the appearance and disappearance of spatial lines as its formal principle — voice separation and voice fusion as dramaturgical poles.

The [ASCOLTA #7 — Artists in Residence](/blog/ascolta/07-ascolta/) session (June 2023) presented several works from ICST residencies in this mode: Nikos Stavropoulos's *Karst Grotto* (Residency 2017/18), recorded in a 5.1 Pentagon configuration, and Diego Ratto's *KOM* (Residency 2018) — both working with clearly differentiated spatial voice-leading. Stavropoulos's *Khemenu* (same session, 1st Prize ISAC-2023) extends the technique into a broader formal architecture.

Mesias Maiguashca: *La Canción de la Tierra* ([ASCOLTA #8](/blog/ascolta/08-ascolta/), ICST Residency 2018–2020) demonstrates a different model of voice separation: **timbral group identity**. Andean instruments, wind ensemble, a twelve-voice choir, and sound objects of wood and metal are positioned as separate spatial groups in the Ambisonics encoding — voice separation through both timbral difference and spatial assignment simultaneously, sustained across 14 sections.

Horacio Vaggione: *Gymel II* ([ASCOLTA #3](/blog/ascolta/03-ascolta/), ICST Residency 2015) offers yet another variant: voice separation through **granularity difference** — eight tracks, morphological figures at varying levels of detail, without timbral contrast as the primary separating mechanism.

### Psychoacoustic Analysis of the Crossing

The critical crossing moment is psychoacoustically complex. The auditory system must decide whether to track two separate trajectories or abandon the continuity of each voice and interpret the next sonic "unit" as a new stream. This capacity for tracking spatial trajectories — auditory stream segregation in movement — depends on the distinguishability of the sounds. If the percussive source and the drone are too similar timbrally, the crossing is perceived as fusion; if they retain sufficient timbral difference, the voice-leading remains readable.

**Compositional consequence:** Crossing moments are only as precise as the timbral differentiation of the voices allows. Space and timbre condition each other here.

Concrete listening examples that make this threshold directly audible: Horacio Vaggione's *Gymel II* ([ASCOLTA #3](/blog/ascolta/03-ascolta/)) is particularly illuminating in this regard — not coincidentally, the piece shares its name with the medieval two-voice technique in which the voices cross. The granularity difference between the eight tracks serves as the primary differentiation parameter: because the voices differ in their microstructural detail, the crossing remains audible as crossing — not as fusion. Mario Mary's *Polyphonic Philosophy* ([ASCOLTA #11](/blog/ascolta/11-ascolta/)) demonstrates the same mechanism on the horizontal plane: at what point does a moving sound-object trajectory tip from "voice" to "texture"? The answer depends directly on movement speed and the timbral distance to the adjacent voice. Juliana Herrero's *FUGA N22* ([3D AudioSpace](https://audiospace.soundingfuture.com/tracks/fuga-n22)) makes the appearance and disappearance of spatial lines its formal principle — what in the fugal tradition constitutes a theme entry is here psychoacoustically precise: a voice only becomes audible when its timbral difference from the surrounding field crosses the segregation threshold. Kim Hedås: *Dense* ([3D AudioSpace](https://audiospace.soundingfuture.com/tracks/dense), EMS — Elektronmusikstudion Stockholm 2017) operates directly on this threshold: sound objects that alternately read as polyphony or as texture — an auditory borderline where the crossing moments cause perception to oscillate between two states.

### Final Shaping: Dissolution or Consolidation

Two possible endings illustrate different compositional decisions:

**Ending A (dissolution):** The three voices converge at a shared spatial point. Their timbral difference decreases. The result is a single diffuse layer — spatial voice-separation dissolves into texture.

**Ending B (consolidation):** The three voices return to clear separation, but in an altered configuration — changed positions, changed spatial roles. This is a structural reprise with transformed spatial syntax.

Both endings are justifiable from the internal logic of the strategy. Ending A shows that voice separation and envelopment are not contradictory strategies but can define a formal transition. Ending B shows that spatial voice-leading can have its own "return dramaturgy."
