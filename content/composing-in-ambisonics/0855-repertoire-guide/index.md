The following overview brings together works from the ASCOLTA programme, ICST residencies, and the [3D AudioSpace platform](https://audiospace.soundingfuture.com/) by Sounding Future — organised by the analytical categories developed in [Work Analyses](/composing-in-ambisonics/085-work-analyses/). Use it alongside the [Spatial Analysis Template](/composing-in-ambisonics/0852-analysis-template/) as a starting point for your own listening practice.

## Spatial Transformation: Object → Field

Philip Samartzis: *Atmospheres & Disturbances* (ICST Residency 2019 / [ASCOLTA #5](/blog/ascolta/05-ascolta/)) follows the transformation of field recordings from the Jungfraujoch into synthetic spatial fields — the prototypical example of the Case Study A dramaturgy. Hans Tutschku: *Remembering Japan*, Part 1 *Immersion* (ICST Residency 2022 / [ASCOLTA #9](/blog/ascolta/09-ascolta/)) shows the same transition in a more concentrated form; the remaining four parts of the cycle each deploy different spatial strategies. Youngjae Cho: *cycle: incarnations – II. narthex* (ICST Residency 2025 / [ASCOLTA #19](/blog/ascolta/19-ascolta/)) works at a lighter material scale: familiarity, destabilisation, acoustic return. Cathy Lane: *The River goes on* (ICST Residency 2022 / [ASCOLTA #5](/blog/ascolta/05-ascolta/)) dissolves field recordings, speech, and archival material into a diffuse memory field. Ágnes Máthé: *Fluidum* ([3D AudioSpace](https://audiospace.soundingfuture.com/tracks/fluidum)) — all sound material from the composer's own water recordings; space is used as a structuring parameter from the earliest stage, with the three-part arc (Arrival — Exploration — Emergence) following the Case Study A dramaturgy: first part clearly detached, second and third mutually driving each other — a model for composing from space outward.

## Spatial Counterpoint and Voice Separation

Mario Mary: *Polyphonic Philosophy* ([ASCOLTA #11](/blog/ascolta/11-ascolta/)) is explicitly conceived as a "melody of sound objects" and "polyphony of space." Mary's *Pedro en su laberinto* (also [ASCOLTA #11](/blog/ascolta/11-ascolta/)) shows the same idea in denser form: multiple trajectories without fusion. Juliana Herrero: *FUGA N22* ([3D AudioSpace](https://audiospace.soundingfuture.com/tracks/fuga-n22)) — fugal voice-leading inspired by John Cage's imaginary landscapes; "open and closed lines in dialogue with space and time." Mesias Maiguashca: *La Canción de la Tierra* ([ASCOLTA #8](/blog/ascolta/08-ascolta/), ICST Residency 2018–2020) — voice separation through timbral group identity: Andean instruments, wind, choir, and sound objects as separate spatial groups across 14 sections. Nikos Stavropoulos: *Karst Grotto* (ICST Residency 2017/18 / [ASCOLTA #7](/blog/ascolta/07-ascolta/)) and *Khemenu* (1st Prize ISAC-2023, same session) — Pentagon recording configuration for spatial voice separation; *Khemenu* extends the technique into a broader formal architecture. Horacio Vaggione: *Gymel II* (ICST Residency 2015 / [ASCOLTA #3](/blog/ascolta/03-ascolta/)) — voice separation through granularity difference across eight tracks. Kim Hedås: *Dense* ([3D AudioSpace](https://audiospace.soundingfuture.com/tracks/dense), EMS — Elektronmusikstudion Stockholm 2017) — sound objects that alternately read as polyphony or texture; a direct encounter with the threshold between the two.

## Envelopment, Depth, and Spectral Space

Natasha Barrett: *Prince Prospero's Party* (ICST Residency 2005 / [ASCOLTA #10](/blog/ascolta/10-ascolta/)) — one of the first pieces in 2nd-order HOA — presents depth stratification between foreground objects and a diffuse spatial field as a clearly audible structural device. Barrett's *Glass Eye* (7th-order HOA, Ultima 2024) and *Kernel Expansion* (5th-order), both available on [3D AudioSpace](https://audiospace.soundingfuture.com/), deploy spectral space as independent compositional material. Jonathan Harvey: *Mortuos plango, vivos voco* ([ASCOLTA #4](/blog/ascolta/04-ascolta/), 8-track, 1980) — voice and bell partials; spectral transformation as spatial formal principle, a standard reference work. Zbigniew Karkowski: *No name* ([ASCOLTA #6](/blog/ascolta/06-ascolta/), ICST studio, 2008) — micro-structured noise with maximum LEV; demonstrates envelopment through reflection density without localisation. Nandele Maguni: *Kampfumo* (ICST Residency 2025 / [ASCOLTA #19](/blog/ascolta/19-ascolta/)) condenses field recordings from Maputo into an acousmatic topography — distance as a social as well as physical dimension.

## Historical Reference Works and Format Comparison

For analyses that include the history of spatial composition, the *Listening Twice* sessions offer a special resource: [ASCOLTA #13](/blog/ascolta/13-ascolta/), [#14](/blog/ascolta/14-ascolta/), and [#15](/blog/ascolta/15-ascolta/) present works in multiple formats (stereo, 5.1, UHJ, HOA), enabling direct comparison analyses — what is lost in reduction, what only becomes possible with the spatial dimension? Jean-Claude Risset: *Resonant Sound Spaces* ([ASCOLTA #4](/blog/ascolta/04-ascolta/)) and Gerald Bennett's portrait programme ([ASCOLTA #2](/blog/ascolta/02-ascolta/)) offer insight into the development of electroacoustic spatial composition from the 1980s to the 2000s.

For extended online listening without a specialised playback space, the [3D AudioSpace platform by Sounding Future](https://audiospace.soundingfuture.com/) makes works accessible in binaurally rendered HOA format — including current competition submissions (SMC 2025, ISAC) and curatorially assembled collections.
